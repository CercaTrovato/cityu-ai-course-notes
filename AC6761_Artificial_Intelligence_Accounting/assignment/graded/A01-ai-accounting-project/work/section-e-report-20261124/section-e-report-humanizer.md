# 4. Investment Recommendation

We recommend HOLD for both Monster and Celsius over a 12–24 month period. We use their closing prices on 19 October 2026: US$46.80 for Monster and US$29.72 for Celsius [5, 6]. Monster has stronger reported profitability and cash generation. Celsius has expanded through acquisitions, but it still needs to turn that growth into steady earnings for ordinary shareholders.

In 2025, Monster's gross margin was 55.8%, compared with 50.4% for Celsius [1, 2]. Monster's management discussion and analysis (MD&A) links its improvement to higher prices and supply-chain measures. Celsius's growth came largely from its acquired brands. Alani Nu and Rockstar contributed US$1,057.5 million of revenue, while revenue from the original CELSIUS brand grew 7.5% [3]. Its operating cash flow also includes the timing effects of distributor payments, so we consider the termination costs still to be paid when assessing its cash generation [2].

We estimate a value per share by multiplying assumed annual earnings per ordinary share (EPS) by a price-to-earnings ratio (P/E). Here, sustainable EPS means earnings that we assume can be repeated each year. A P/E of 40 means paying US$40 for each US$1 of annual EPS. Monster's central case is therefore US$1.10 × 40 = US$44.00 per share. The table uses the same assumptions as the presentation.

Monster's central value is 6.0% below the reference price. We give it a higher central P/E of 40 because we judge its business quality to be stronger. Its 2025 diluted EPS was US$1.94, or US$0.97 after adjusting for the two-for-one split [1, 4]. Our central EPS of US$1.10 assumes further earnings growth. At the reference price, we do not see enough of a discount to recommend BUY. A lower price or stronger recurring earnings would make it more attractive. Falling margins and weaker cash generation would weaken our HOLD recommendation.

Celsius's central value is just 0.9% above its reference price. This case also depends on EPS recovering to US$1.20 from its 2025 GAAP diluted EPS of US$0.25. Management reported adjusted EPS of US$1.34, which is a separate non-GAAP measure [3]. Our US$1.20 assumption depends on the recovery becoming sustainable. We keep HOLD because the larger brand portfolio offers growth opportunities, while integration, changes in distribution and preferred-share claims add risk [2]. We would reconsider the rating after stronger brand demand and recurring earnings, together with cash generation after settlements. Continued integration problems or weak cash conversion could support SELL.

# 5. Critical Evaluation of AI Methodology

The group draft describes using AI to find reports, extract financial figures, write calculation code, compare sections of the annual reports and prepare the first draft. It records two problems: a report belonged to the wrong company, and text could not be extracted properly from a PDF. Checking the company and year helps avoid the first error. Using the official HTML filing and inspecting the statement pages helps with the second.

We also need to distinguish between accounting measures. Consolidated net income and income available to common shareholders are different measures. Celsius reports its convertible preferred stock as mezzanine equity, separately from liabilities and common shareholders' equity [2]. Monster's share price and EPS also need to use the same split basis [4]. These choices affect which figures belong in a ratio.

AI can help organize tables and compare themes across reports. We can check numerical outputs against the original figures and calculations. Text analysis involves more judgement. Management may use positive language to present the company favourably, and selected passages may leave out other evidence. We therefore need to read the original passages and compare them with the financial results before accepting a tone label.

Our valuation depends on the EPS and P/E assumptions. AI helps calculate each case, while financial evidence is needed to justify the inputs. The draft lists the checks but does not provide the full workflow logs.
