What Regression Can Tell Us


   For information xt−1 known before forecast month t:

                                        rt = α + βxt−1 + εt .



   ▶ α is the ﬁtted monthly market return when xt−1 = 0.
   ▶ β is the ﬁtted change in next-month return for a one-unit change in xt−1 .
   ▶ εt is the part not summarized by this line.
   ▶ The regression describes conditional association in this sample.

   Questions that still remain
   Is the relation causal, stable, tradable, or useful in a genuinely new period?

Reading the Standard Error


   For the momentum slope:

                  Coeﬃcient      Estimate    Standard error    t statistic   p-value
                  Momentum          0.6 pp           2.1 pp          0.31       0.76


   ▶ A standard error describes how much an estimate would vary across repeated samples
     generated by the same process.
   ▶ The estimate is small relative to its sampling uncertainty.
   ▶ A large p-value is not proof that the true relation is exactly zero.
   ▶ A small p-value would not prove stability, causality, or economic value.
   ▶ Monthly ﬁnancial residuals may still violate the simplest standard-error assumptions.

Multiple Regression
Why Add More Than One Predictor?


                rt = α + β1 Momentumt−1 + β2 Volatilityt−1 + β3 MA gapt−1 + εt .



   ▶ A simple slope mixes the predictor’s own relation with correlated variables.
   ▶ Multiple regression asks how returns vary with one predictor while holding the others ﬁxed.
   ▶ Adding controls can change a slope’s sign and magnitude.
   ▶ More predictors also increase estimation noise and researcher discretion.

   Keep in mind
   Putting every available predictor into one regression is the kitchen sink of Welch and Goyal
   (2008 RFS). In their data it ﬁts the sample best and loses to the historical mean out of
   sample by a wide margin. Today’s three-predictor model is a small kitchen sink; Class 3 keeps
   the sink and adds a penalty.

                                                                                 30/56
Correlated Predictors Make Individual Slopes Unstable

   ▶ What you see: a coeﬃcient changes sign or size between the simple and the multiple
     regression, its standard error widens, and yet R 2 barely moves.
   ▶ Why: when two predictors move together the data cannot say which one carries the eﬀect,
     so the ﬁtted line is well determined while the split between them is not.
   ▶ How to check: reﬁt on a diﬀerent subsample, or drop one of the pair, and see whether the
     surviving coeﬃcients stay where they were.
   ▶ What to do here: report the forecast rather than the individual slope, and stop treating
     one coeﬃcient as the ﬁnding.

   Keep in mind
   Correlated predictors are not a defect in the data. They are the normal state of ﬁnancial
   predictors, and Class 3 answers them directly: shrink the slopes, or replace them with a few
   combinations.

How We Will Judge a Return Forecast




   No single statistic settles whether returns are predictable.

   1. Sample association: slope, uncertainty, and in-sample R 2 .
   2. Forecast error: MSE, RMSE, and MAE in untouched observations.
   3. Relative accuracy: improvement over a stated benchmark.
   4. Direction: whether predicted and realized signs match, relative to the base rate.
   5. Stability: whether results persist through time and across reasonable designs.
   6. Economic value: whether a feasible portfolio beneﬁts after costs, which Class 5 evaluates.

When the Regression Output Looks Wrong



  Use this order:

   1. print the exact columns and ﬁrst ﬁve regression rows;
   2. conﬁrm target and predictor dates by hand for three rows;
   3. check missing values, duplicates, units, and extreme observations;
   4. ﬁt the simplest one-predictor model;
   5. compare two Python implementations or compute the slope by formula;
   6. only then add transformations or more predictors.

  Start with dates, rows, and units before changing the model.
