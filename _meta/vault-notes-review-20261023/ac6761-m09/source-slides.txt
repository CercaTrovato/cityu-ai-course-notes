Use of AI in Accounting
Identifying: Optical Character Recognition (OCR) + Natural Language Processing (NLP)
Read invoices, receipts and bank statements, and turn paper into data.
Recording: Robotic Process Automation (RPA)

Use of AI in stakeholders
Investors: Assess company performance to make informed decisions. Financial statements indicate profitability, growth potential, and risks, affecting stock prices and dividends.
Creditors: Analyze liquidity and solvency ratios to evaluate the firm's ability to meet obligations. Strong financials suggest lower lending risk.
Suppliers: Evaluate financial stability to gauge the risk of extending credit. A financially healthy company is more likely to pay on time, ensuring stable partnerships.
Financial Analysts: Offer insights and recommendations for investments and financial planning, influencing market perceptions and decisions.
Auditors: Examine financial statements for accuracy and compliance, asset risks, and provide recommendation to ensure transparency and integrity in financial reporting.

PAGE 16
Use of AI in stakeholders
Management: Use financial analysis to assess performance, make strategic decisions, and plan for growth, identifying strengths and weaknesses to drive improvements.
Employees: Assess the company's financial health to gauge job security, salaries, and benefits. Concerns about profitability and stability directly impact their livelihoods.
Customers: Seek assurance about the company’s stability, especially in long-term contracts. A financially sound firm is more likely to meet commitments and provide reliable products or services.

Financial statement analyses - stakeholders
Regulators: Analyze financial statements to ensure compliance with laws and regulations, maintaining market integrity and protecting public interests.
Competitors: Benchmark performance and strategize based on market financial health. Understanding competitors’ financial positions aids in pricing and market positioning.
Community: Examine financial statements to evaluate corporate responsibility. Financial stability indicates a firm's capacity to invest in sustainable practices and community initiatives.

AI hallucination - definition
What is Hallucination?: Hallucination in AI refers to instances where a model generates information that is incorrect, fabricated, or nonsensical. This can include producing made-up statistics, facts, or even entire narratives that have no basis in reality.
Why It Happens: AI models, especially large language models, are trained on vast datasets that contain both accurate and inaccurate information. When generating responses, the model may mix elements from different sources, leading to errors.

PAGE 34
AI hallucination - examples
Fictional Data: An AI might claim that a specific audit identified a non-existent financial anomaly or provide fictitious numbers while discussing financial performance.
Incorrect References: An AI could reference studies or documents that do not actually exist or misattribute quotes to incorrect sources.

PAGE 35
AI hallucination - impact
Trust Issues: Hallucination can erode trust in AI systems. Users may become skeptical of AI outputs, leading to reliance on traditional methods that may be less efficient.
Financial Consequences: In fields like accounting, inaccurate information can lead to miscalculations, faulty reports, and ultimately poor business decisions or compliance issues.

PAGE 36
AI hallucination - mitigation
Cross-Verification: Always validate AI-generated information against authoritative sources. Encourage students to fact-check outputs before use.
Human Oversight: Highlight the importance of human judgment. AI should assist professionals, not replace them; human expertise is critical in evaluating AI outputs.
Model Training Improvements: Discuss ongoing research aimed at improving AI models to reduce hallucination rates. This includes using more curated datasets and enhancing algorithms.

AI hallucination – user education
Awareness: Educate students about the limitations of AI. Make it clear that while AI can provide valuable insights, it is not infallible.
Critical Thinking: Encourage students to develop critical thinking skills. They should question AI outputs and apply their knowledge to assess the validity of the information.

AI hallucination – future
Research and Development: Highlight advancements in AI research focused on reducing hallucinations, such as developing models that can explain their reasoning and uncertainty.
Transparency and Explainability: Stress the importance of transparent AI systems that provide context for their outputs, making it easier for users to understand and verify information.
